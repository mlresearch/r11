abstract: Popular Monte-Carlo tree search (MCTS) al- gorithms for online planning,
  such as $\varepsilon$-greedy tree search and UCT, aim at rapidly iden- tifying a
  reasonably good action, but pro- vide rather poor worst-case guarantees on performance
  improvement over time. In con- trast, a recently introduced MCTS algorithm BRUE
  guarantees exponential-rate improve- ment over time, yet it is not geared towards
  identifying reasonably good choices right at the go. We take a stand on the individual
  strengths of these two classes of algorithms, and show how they can be effectively
  con- nected. We then rationalize a principle of “selective tree expansion”, and
  suggest a con- crete implementation of this principle within MCTS. The resulting
  algorithms favorably compete with other MCTS algorithms under short planning times,
  while preserving the at- tractive convergence properties of BRUE.
