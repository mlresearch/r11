abstract: Reasoning about degrees of belief in uncertain dynamic worlds is fundamental
  to many applications, such as robotics and planning, where actions modify state
  properties and sensors provide measurements, both of which are prone to noise. With
  the exception of limited cases such as Gaussian processes over linear phenomena,
  belief state evolution can be complex and hard to reason with in a general way.
  This paper proposes a framework with new results that allows the reduction of
  subjective probabilities after sensing and acting, both in discrete and continuous
  domains, to questions about the initial state only. We build on an expressive probabilistic
  first-order logical account by Bacchus, Halpern and Levesque, resulting in a methodology
  that, in principle, can be coupled with a variety of existing inference solutions.
